cost sufficiency. None occurred.

What does the completed panel nevertheless say about policy versus cost?

## Policy signal

Upper-cost and Candidate-F both die in all twelve blocks, so survival alone
cannot separate them.

Lived time does separate them directionally:

- upper-cost longer than Candidate-F: **7 blocks**;
- shorter: 1;
- tied: 4.

This is not a sign-test claim and the 12 blocks are not independent populations.

The difference is accompanied by changed physical behavior:

- Candidate-F: 79 moves, 4 positive water uptakes;
- upper-cost P1.3-choice: 216 moves, 25 positive water uptakes.

The Candidate-F shadow and actual P1.3 motor action differ on **510/2395**
upper-cost decisions.

In the four opportunities where reference P1.3 reaches horizon, Candidate-F
never obtains water. The upper-cost P1.3-choice arm obtains water in all four
and lives longer than Candidate-F in all four, though it still dies.

This pattern is difficult to reconcile with a claim that motor policy is
irrelevant.

## Cost signal

The upper-cost intervention is intentionally severe:

- it runs Candidate-F shadow cognition;
- physically includes that work;
- then runs and pays P1.3 choice;
- only P1.3 controls the action.

It accumulates 868 P1.3 `NO_PROCESSING_BUDGET` steps and 36.24% physical
inactivity. Candidate-F itself has 26.96% inactivity; reference has 14.81%.

Upper-cost paid energy/vigilance per lived step is 1.66955 / 2.11048, versus
reference 0.60876 / 0.75158. Candidate-F's realized rates are still higher at
2.06095 / 2.90923 because trajectories and option sets diverge. Therefore
"upper-cost" describes the **mechanistic billing rule**, not a guarantee that
the realized per-step bill on a different trajectory exceeds Candidate-F.

No cost-matching claim is valid.

## Death transition

Upper-cost deaths: 8 dehydration, 4 starvation.

Reference deaths among the eight non-survivors: 5 dehydration, 3 starvation.

The upper-cost shift from water deaths toward food deaths is consistent with

causal endpoint.

## What is falsified and what is not

Not observed:
- a strict upper-survives / Candidate-dies block.

Therefore **not falsified**:
- universal cost sufficiency, under the preregistered strict criterion.

But the panel also does **not support** the opposite statement that policy is
irrelevant. Directional lifespan, movement, water uptake and 510 shadow-vs-motor
choice disagreements all show substantial policy consequences.

The appropriate conclusion is narrower:

> The overcharged control is too harsh to isolate survival mediation, while its
> sub-horizon behavior provides clear evidence that retaining P1.3 motor policy
