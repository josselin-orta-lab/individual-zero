## Explanation boundary

The local synthetic 48-Mind probe showed that TD priority and random memory identity produce different immediate learned states at equal prepaid replay count. This new full-life observation shows **negative TD-priority horizon survival on its finite matched DEVELOPMENT panel**, not a confirmed causal mechanism that priority hurts survival. The intervention changes memory identities and can then alter the individual's experienced future, cognitive demand, sleep, count actually paid and risk; equal **count decision rule** does not mean identical realized replay counts. No reward redesign, imposed survival behavior or adaptive favorable seed selection was introduced.

This panel had fixed n=48, a descriptive endpoint, no internally/externally attested random ecological superpopulation, no independent survival inference review and no previously justified superiority/NI threshold. Its observed result is not a statistically established generalized superiority of RANDOM or inferiority of TD priority. Do not infer survival equivalence from null strata or treat a favorable ecological subset as a separate confirmation.

## Research decision

**RETAIN NEGATIVE TD-PRIORITY SIGNAL / CLOSE SOURCE AND RUNNER / NO SCIENTIFIC GO.** All 48 matched World/G0/life units were consumed exactly once. The original executor and its main-branch launch trigger must be hard-disabled; never reuse its seeds or edit original evidence. Next scientific intervention is source-bound independent original gzip verification, then investigation of *when* priority causes systematically different learning and physiological trajectories. A later crossed design can distinguish adaptive replay **count** from replay **content**, using new sources only. ## Post-hoc only: water-death discordance observation (not a new hypothesis test)

After observing the primary finite-panel result, a read-only review of all 96 original reduced rows grouped pairs by their terminal outcome. In the **7 RANDOM-CONTENT-only survivor pairs**, the corresponding TD-PRIORITY individual died from `zero_water` in every case (depletion 4, pulse 2, stable 1). In the **1 PRIORITY-only survivor pair**, the corresponding random-content individual also died from `zero_water` (stable). In the **24 pairs dying under both policies**, each arm recorded 23 deaths due to `zero_water` and one due to `zero_food`. No such death cause occurred in the 16 both-survivor pairs. These are descriptions *conditioned on the observed outcome*, not independent randomized evidence that replay content affected water acquisition.

Within **16 both-survivor pairs**, TD priority paid on average **20.2423 less energy** and **24.3264 less vigilance** per assigned life than random content, despite executing on average **107.125 more replays**. In the 7 RANDOM-CONTENT-only survivor pairs, priority lived on average **70 fewer steps**, and therefore paid much less cumulative cognition. These are post-outcome-selected groups: differences in survival duration are a major confounder. Do not report the aggregate energy difference as intrinsic replay computational efficiency.

The original run did not retain a complete step-by-step decision trace (`keep_trace=False`); it is impossible to reconstruct an exact first water-belief or water-action divergence for these eight discordant pairs from the available reduced data. **Never regenerate the exhausted cohort to obtain such a trace.** The next *prospectively frozen*, new-World study should test whether replay content changes the development of self-acquired information about water and subsequent choices, recording only paid/nonintrusive observer readouts at a declared cadence, with full physical costs and all adverse outcomes. It must not prescribe water-seeking behavior, alter priority weights after seeing these signs, or claim a causal survival mechanism from this post-hoc stratification. See [issue #1258](https://github.com/josselin-orta-lab/individual-zero/issues/1258).
