## Same-event proper score

For every original factual next-body delta `y` and pre-outcome native EffectsModel mean `p`, five-channel mean squared loss `L_native = mean((y-p)^2)`. The post-hoc **zero-change** comparator's loss on the **same** outcome is `L_zero = mean(y^2)`. Positive event gain `L_zero-L_native` favors native. The comparator needs no extra inference and **is not matched to native cognitive operator work**. Neither forecast is delivered to a new controller; factual action and World outcomes are identical by construction of this offline analysis.

With four technical lives in two familiar developer Worlds, no credible general-population confidence interval, significance test or inference margin is available. ### Tail-error diagnostic: large learned-model misses matter

In the original fixed source, the five **largest** negative same-event gains all occur on native `boire` actions with previously seen action keys. The worst individual event is **dev-open-field / 840061, step 66**, exact-key count 3: zero-change loss **1.089327**, native loss **99.161171**, difference **-98.071844**. The next worst same-unit event, step 70, has zero-change **0.577970**, native **61.250168**, gain **-60.672197**. The top five adverse events account descriptively for roughly **45.8%** of the sum of all negative event gains in the fixed sample. This analysis does not establish why they occur: source depletion, clipping, fatigue, wrong action effect and changing World conditions are untested explanations, not conclusions. The individual experienced no new World/action in this reanalysis.

A learned model may be more accurate in **frequency** while having worse mean score in some full trajectories because occasional errors are large. It follows that a future decision-to-think policy cannot justify payment from a count proxy or hit frequency alone. It must estimate the **consequences of uncertainty, error tails and opportunity cost**, using the individual's existing body stakes and actual learned data, not a laboratory survival-reward rule.

## Answer, gate and bounded stop

**Supported:** native first-person learned effect forecasts already exist, can be prospectively frozen and properly scored on factual bodies, and are often better than an unaided zero-change extrapolation on a per-event basis. The strong adverse-unit/tail heterogeneity in the same source shows this cannot yet be generalized or converted into an automatic value-of-computation signal.

**Not supported:** a reliable conditional confidence interval from the existing `1/sqrt(1+n_exact)` proxy; evidence that a new extra computation has marginal value; a causal learned-vs-unlearned model effect; an equal-work VISIBLE/PAID_DISCARD next native motor difference; autonomous choice to buy extra cognition; body-adjusted net benefit; independent ecological generalization or Stage-2 closure. The baseline is cheaper than extra cognition and never participates in a real-world action contrast.

**Decision:** preserve the negative and positive finite data, do **not** invent an action-specific `boire` override or retune E3 around the identified tail cases. **Stop additional adaptive-controller candidates** without a distinct prospectively justified first-person information source and a properly precommitted, paid, no-World native-action falsifier. The source-specific next methodological task is to assess the **validity of the existing uncertainty proxy and severe predictive tails** on truly independent histories/Worlds when authorized; do not use retrospective data to certify that calibration. `Stage 2.1 NOT_ISSUED`, P1.3 funded-v2 reference, I1 active registered, no Stage 2.2/2.3 GO, old scientific Worlds sealed.
