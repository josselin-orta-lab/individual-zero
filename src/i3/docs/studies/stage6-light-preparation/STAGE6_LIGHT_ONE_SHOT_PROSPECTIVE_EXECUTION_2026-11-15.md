## Immutable scientific question

Does persistence-only rank-weighted parental selection produce a reproducible *directional* displacement of the heritable `beta_u_eff` gene expression after two selection generations, relative to a score-blind uniform random-parent arm? Unit of inference: each of **16 independently seeded populations** of 24 founders, not each of the 11,520 lived evaluations. G0 is common and occurs once, six F0 ecological opportunities per generation: stable, depletion and pulse, two repeats. The fixed rank weights are 24 down to 1, the mutation standard deviation is 0.1 of the range for each bounded genome-v4 gene. The only fitness inputs are sealed censored-survivor count, total steps alive and slot tiebreak. No conditioning on future Worlds, genotypes, cognitive costs or observer-reported quality.

At G2 the frozen contrast for population p is:
```
C[p] = mean_24(beta_u_eff, selected, p, G2) - mean_24(beta_u_eff, random_parent, p, G2)
beta_u_eff = beta_u * (1 + .5 * temperament.exploration)
```
The two-sided threshold is **13 or more concordant signs of 16** in *either direction*, with zero contrasts neither positive nor negative, retaining n=16. Under independent symmetric nonzero signs, the exact two-sided tail probability at 13 is 1394/65536 ≈ 0.02127, not a claim of universal survival advantage. Three predeclared neutral sentinels are `omega`, `temperament.affiliation`, `temperament.reward_sensitivity`. Two or more concordant sentinels invalidate the result; one must be disclosed as an alert. All observations including deaths, censored lives and null or unfavorable effects are retained, regardless of the outcome status.

The historical P3 four-population G2 `beta_u_eff` contrasts were negative in all four, mean -0.375246. They were descriptive, with naive two-sided p=0.125, and cannot justify retuning sign direction or classifying any new trial as positive.
